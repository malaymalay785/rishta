Things to Discuss Before Marriage

Many problems in married life start because two people never talked about important things before the wedding. Talking early can feel awkward, but it is one of the kindest things you can do for each other. This guide lists topics and gentle questions to help you.

How to start these talks

Do not ask everything on the first day. First build a little trust through normal conversation. Then bring up one topic at a time, in a calm and polite voice. Say your own view first, and then ask for theirs. Avoid sounding like an interview. It is also wise to let your families be part of the bigger topics, such as where to live and how to share responsibilities. Remember that the aim is to understand each other, not to win an argument.

Not every difference is a problem. Two people can think differently about small things and still have a good marriage. What matters is whether you can talk about the big things with respect.

Family expectations

In Pakistan, marriage affects the whole family. It is important to know what each family expects.

  • How often will you meet or live with parents and relatives?
  • Will you celebrate family events together, and how will you share time between both families?
  • What role will parents have in big decisions?
  • How will you handle disagreements between family members?

Be honest about your duties to your parents. A good partner will understand that you love your family, as long as there is balance and respect.

Education and career

  • Does each person plan to work after marriage?
  • Does anyone want to study more, or change jobs?
  • What work hours, travel or night shifts should the other person expect?
  • If one person wants to work and the other does not, how will you settle this?

These questions should be asked for both men and women. Respect each person's dreams and plan together how to support them.

Location after marriage

This is a very common cause of stress if it is left unclear. Talk about which city or country you will live in. If one of you lives abroad, ask how the move, visa and family visits will be handled and how long it may take. Ask whether either person is ready to move, and for how long. Be honest even if the answer is not what the other person hopes to hear. On Rishta PK, there is a field about willingness to move abroad or to another city, but you should still discuss it in detail.

Financial expectations

Money talk is not rude. It is a part of planning a home. Calmly discuss:

  • How will household costs be shared?
  • Will there be a joint account, or will each person keep some money for themselves?
  • Does anyone have loans or must support parents, brothers or sisters?
  • How much do you want to save, and for what?
  • What are your views on spending, gifts and wedding costs?

Wedding costs deserve special care. Agree early on a simple plan that does not put either family into debt or under pressure. Never agree to anything that you cannot honestly afford.

Living arrangements

Will you live in a joint family or in a separate home? Who will take care of elder parents? How much privacy do you both need? Who will handle which household tasks? Be open about your expectations and about what feels fair. A plan that both people agree to is better than a silent expectation that causes hurt later.

Children

  • Do you both want children, and about when?
  • How many children would you like?
  • How would you share the care, school choices and costs?
  • If one person already has children, how will the new family live together?

If you or your partner have children from an earlier marriage, talk about this gently and honestly, and think about the feelings of the children too.

Responsibilities

Marriage is a partnership. Talk about who will do what at home, who will take care of sick relatives, and how decisions will be made. Today many couples share work in a flexible way, instead of fixed roles. Find a style that suits both of you and that both families can accept. Agree that both will listen and help when the other is tired or in trouble.

Lifestyle and habits

Talk openly about everyday habits: sleep time, food, friends, social media use, travel, free time, hobbies, and habits such as smoking. Small daily habits can cause big frustration if they are very different. You do not have to be the same, but you should know what to expect and respect each other's space.

Religious and cultural expectations

Ask how important faith is in daily life. For example, talk about prayer, fasting, dress, and how you will celebrate festivals. Also talk about cultural customs, language and traditions from each family. Ask with respect, listen without judging, and look for what you share. If you have different views, ask yourselves whether you can respect them calmly in a long marriage.

Long-term goals

  • Where do you see yourselves in five and ten years?
  • Do you want to buy a home or start a business?
  • How do you plan to care for parents when they are older?
  • What do you want your home to feel like?

Shared goals do not have to be identical. But they should not pull you in opposite directions.

How to handle disagreements

You will not agree on everything, and that is normal. Notice how the other person behaves when you disagree. Do they listen, stay calm and try to find a middle way? Or do they shout, mock or go silent for days? How a person handles disagreement before marriage often shows how they will after marriage. Take any big concern to a trusted elder or family member for advice. Also read our guide on how to find the right marriage partner for more on compatibility and red flags.

Be honest and take your time

Do not hide important facts, such as health matters, past marriages, debts or family responsibilities. Honesty at the start is better than a shock later. It is fine to take several conversations over weeks to cover these topics. If you feel pressured to decide quickly, slow down.
