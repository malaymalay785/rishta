Safety and Security on Rishta PK

Meeting people online can be a good way to start a rishta, but it needs care. This page explains the safety tools inside Rishta PK and the simple habits that help you protect yourself and your family.

No website can promise that every person is honest. Rishta PK gives you tools to protect yourself, but your own care and your family's advice are the most important protection.

Safety tools inside Rishta PK

  • Google sign in: every member signs in with a Google account, so there is no shared password to steal.
  • Friend requests first: you can chat only after the other person accepts your request. Nobody can send you messages without your approval.
  • Report: every profile card has a Report button. Use it when a profile looks fake, rude or unsafe.
  • Block: blocking hides the person from you and hides you from them. You can unblock later in Settings.
  • Unfriend: inside a chat you can unfriend a person. The chat is removed for both of you.
  • Delete account: you can delete your account in Settings. Your profile is hidden at once and your data is permanently deleted after 15 days.

Protect your personal information

Your profile is visible to other members, so share only what you are comfortable with. Think before you write anything in your profile or in a chat.

  • Do not write your full home address, your exact workplace or your daily routine.
  • Do not share your CNIC, passport, bank details or card numbers. A genuine rishta never needs them at the start.
  • Keep your Google password private. Never share a code that arrives on your phone or email.
  • Use a clear photo of yourself, but think about what is visible behind you, such as house numbers or school names.

Be careful with phone numbers and documents

Rishta PK has a chat so you can talk first without giving your phone number. Move to phone or video calls only when you feel comfortable, and ideally after a trusted family member knows about the conversation. Do not send private photos, documents or family papers to someone you have not met through your families. If someone keeps asking for them, that is a warning sign.

Never send money to strangers

This is the most important rule. Do not send money, mobile load, gift cards or bank transfers to anyone you met online, whatever the reason. A person may say they have an emergency, a visa problem, a customs fee or a business loss. A person who truly wants a marriage will not make money the first topic. If anyone asks, stop the conversation, block the profile and report it.

Avoid scams and fake profiles

Some people create fake profiles to cheat others. These signs should make you slow down:

  • The photo looks like a model or a film star, or the person refuses to share any other picture.
  • The person says "I love you" very fast, or wants to talk about marriage within a day or two.
  • The details in the profile do not match what the person says in chat.
  • The person asks you to leave Rishta PK and talk on another app straight away.
  • You receive links to click, files to install or forms asking for your details.

Verify before big decisions

A profile is only the first introduction. Before any serious step, check the facts together with your family. Ask about education, job and family in a polite way, and meet through relatives or common acquaintances where possible. Take your time. A person who is honest will understand why you want to confirm things. The blue tick on Rishta PK means the profile has been marked as verified by us, but you should still use your own judgment.

Report and block when needed

If someone makes you uncomfortable, you do not have to explain yourself or continue the chat.

  1. Tap Report on the profile card and write a short, clear reason. Our team reviews reports.
  2. Tap Block if you do not want to see the person again.
  3. If you are already friends, open the chat and use Unfriend.
  4. Keep a screenshot if someone threatened or tried to cheat you. If you feel in danger or lost money, contact the police or the cybercrime authorities in your country.

Meeting someone in real life

In Pakistani culture, the first meeting usually happens with the family, which is also the safest way. Whether you are a man or a woman, these habits help:

  • Tell a trusted family member who you are meeting, where and when.
  • Meet in a public place or at home with family present. Do not go to a private or far place.
  • Arrange your own transport and keep your phone charged.
  • Do not accept food or drink you are unsure about, and leave if something feels wrong.
  • Do not feel forced to agree on anything on the first meeting.

Involve trusted family members

Parents, siblings and elders have seen many rishtas and can notice things you may miss. You do not have to tell them every message, but share who you are talking to and what you are planning.
