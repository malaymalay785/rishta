Pakistani Family Rishta Guide

In Pakistan, a rishta is not only a bond between two people. It brings two families together. This guide is for parents, elders and relatives who help in the search, and also for young people who want to include their family in a good way. It gives calm, practical points and avoids strict rules, because every family is different.

Why family involvement matters

Parents and elders have life experience. They can notice things that a young person may miss, such as how a family treats others, or whether the details they heard are consistent. Family support also gives comfort and safety, especially when people meet through a website. At the same time, the person who will live the marriage must feel heard. The best results come when the young person and the family decide together.

Using a website as a family

Some families create a profile together, and some parents sit with their son or daughter when they browse. This is fine, as long as the profile is honest and the young person agrees to everything that is written. On Rishta PK, every member signs in with their own Google account and must be 18 or older. Parents can help with choosing words and with checking facts, but the profile should represent the real person. Our guide on how to create a good rishta profile explains what to write.

Compatibility: what to look at

Families often look at education, job, city, family background and religion. These are useful, but they are not everything. Also think about character, kindness, habits, and how the two people speak and listen. Ask yourself:

  • Do both people share similar values about faith, family and respect?
  • Are their life plans compatible, such as work, location and children?
  • Do they seem to enjoy talking to each other and treat each other kindly?
  • Are both families comfortable with each other's way of life?

Do not judge a person by stereotypes about a city, caste, profession or group. Every person is different, and a person should be judged by their own behaviour and honesty.

Communication between families

The first contact between families sets the tone for the future. Keep it warm and simple. Introduce yourselves, ask about the family in a polite way, and be ready to answer the same questions honestly. Avoid comparing the other family with others, avoid sharing private matters with outsiders, and do not speak badly about other proposals. Be clear and kind if you want to say no. A short polite message such as "Thank you for your time, we feel this is not the right match for us" is enough. You do not need to give a long explanation.

Education and career

Education and work are important for stable family life, but they should be discussed in a balanced way. Ask what the person does, what they plan, and how they see their future. Respect the dreams of both sons and daughters, including their wish to study or work after marriage, and talk about it openly instead of assuming. Look for effort and good character as well as titles.

Expectations on both sides

Many rishtas fail because of unspoken expectations. Talk about them politely and early, for example:

  • Where the couple will live, and how often they will visit parents.
  • Roles in the household and respect for each person's work.
  • Which customs matter to each family and which can be kept simple.
  • What each family thinks about contact between the couple before the wedding.

For a longer list of topics, see things to discuss before marriage.

Financial discussions

Money talks are sensitive, so handle them with care and respect. It is fine to ask about the person's work and general stability. Avoid treating marriage like a business deal. Discuss wedding costs openly, and agree on a simple plan that does not push either family into debt. Do not make demands for gifts or money, and do not accept pressure to give more than your family can afford. A marriage that begins with fairness and honesty has a better chance of staying peaceful.

Avoid unnecessary pressure

Pressure can come from relatives, neighbours or the fear of "what people will say". It can also come from age, which makes many families rush. Please remember that a rushed decision can cause years of hardship. Do not force a son or daughter to say yes, and do not use emotional words such as "you will break our heart" to make someone agree. Give young people space to say no, and respect their answer. In the same way, young people should listen to their parents' worries with patience.

Safety: families can help here too

When you meet someone through a website, families can add a strong layer of safety. Check the details together, meet in a safe place with family present, and never send money or share private documents early. If anyone asks for money, pushes you to keep things secret, or avoids meeting the family, pause and think. Read our online rishta safety tips and our safety and security page. On Rishta PK, you can use Report, Block and Unfriend whenever something feels wrong.

Taking enough time

A good decision usually needs several steps: reading the profile, a polite chat, a talk between the families, a meeting in a safe place, asking relatives for their opinion and time for prayer and thought. There is no fixed number of weeks that is right for everyone. What matters is that both people and both families feel comfortable and informed. If something is unclear, ask. If something feels wrong, wait. It is better to take a few more weeks than to carry a regret for many years.

A short checklist for families

  • Is the information about the person honest and consistent?
  • Does the young person feel comfortable and free to say yes or no?
  • Have we talked politely about location, work, money and family roles?
  • Are we free from pressure, comparisons and stereotypes?
  • Have we met in a safe way and taken enough time?
